## 1. Introducing LuminaFM

### What is LuminaFM?
LuminaFM is being developed as a modern, practical software platform designed specifically for facility and maintenance teams. Its primary purpose is to help organizations manage their physical buildings, facilities, equipment, daily maintenance routines, and technical personnel within a single, well-organized system.

In many commercial and industrial facilities today, critical maintenance information remains fragmented. Equipment manuals sit in forgotten filing cabinets, daily operating logs are scribbled into paper notebooks, preventive maintenance schedules live in isolated spreadsheets, and emergency repairs are coordinated through chaotic phone calls and messaging chats. When an asset breaks down, maintenance supervisors often have to reconstruct its history from memory or sift through stacks of weathered paper.

LuminaFM aims to replace this scattered approach with a unified, transparent digital workspace.

### The Core Vision
The fundamental vision behind LuminaFM is simple: **Maintenance should not be reactive guesswork.** 

Every piece of equipment tells a story through its daily temperatures, operating pressures, running hours, and subtle physical symptoms. When this operational information is recorded consistently and connected directly to maintenance work orders, maintenance managers can:
- Spot deteriorating equipment condition before catastrophic failure occurs.
- Ensure routine preventive servicing is never overlooked.
- Understand the true lifecycle costs and reliability of every machine.
- Make confident, evidence-based maintenance and replacement decisions.

## 2. The Problems We Want to Solve

Facility managers and maintenance engineers face demanding daily challenges. Keeping complex facilities running smoothly, safely, and cost-effectively is an immense responsibility. Through industry observation and ongoing maintenance research, we have identified several common challenges that LuminaFM aims to address:

### Common Industry Maintenance Challenges

1. **Scattered and Fragile Maintenance Records**  
   Maintenance logs, equipment manuals, past repair notes, and warranty certificates are often stored across physical binders, loose paper sheets, and disconnected spreadsheets. Important historical records are easily lost, misplaced, or damaged.

2. **Difficulty Tracking Equipment History**  
   When a machine malfunctions, technicians rarely have immediate access to its complete history. Knowing when it was last serviced, what parts were replaced, who performed the work, and whether the same symptom occurred six months ago requires tedious investigation.

3. **Missed Preventive Maintenance Tasks**  
   When preventive maintenance schedules rely on memory, wall calendars, or static spreadsheets, routine inspections and filter changes can easily be postponed or forgotten during busy operational periods—eventually leading to preventable breakdowns.

4. **Manual and Disjointed Work Coordination**  
   Work requests, job assignments, and progress updates are frequently handled verbally or through informal messaging. Without a centralized tracking board, managers struggle to know which jobs are active, which are delayed, and which have been verified as complete.

5. **Delayed Response to Early Warning Signs**  
   Machines rarely fail without warning. An unusual vibration, a slight temperature rise, or a drop in operating pressure usually precedes a breakdown. When shift logs are kept on paper, these warning trends remain invisible until the equipment suddenly stops.

6. **Loss of Institutional and Operational Wisdom**  
   Experienced operators and senior technicians possess invaluable practical knowledge about the idiosyncrasies of specific machines. When these senior staff members retire or change jobs, decades of operational wisdom walk out the door with them.

7. **Limited Visibility into Maintenance Costs and Labor**  
   Facility executives often find it difficult to determine the exact annual cost of maintaining a specific asset, how many labor hours were spent on corrective repairs versus preventive care, or whether an aging machine has become uneconomical to repair.

8. **Maintenance Decisions Based on Intuition Rather than Evidence**  
   Decisions about which equipment to overhaul, what spare parts to stock, or how to allocate limited maintenance budgets are often made under pressure using gut feeling rather than verifiable operational data.

### How a Structured Platform Helps
LuminaFM is designed to eliminate these blind spots. By consolidating asset records, shift inspection logs, scheduled servicing routines, and corrective repair histories into one accessible system, the platform creates an indelible operational memory for the facility. Every team member—from the plant operator recording morning gauge readings to the Chief Engineer reviewing quarterly maintenance budgets—works from the same clear, reliable picture.

## 3. How LuminaFM Will Work

LuminaFM supports the natural day-to-day workflow of a professional maintenance department. The entire platform is structured around a ten-step general maintenance process:

```
[1. Register Equipment] ──► [2. Record Specifications] ──► [3. Create PM Schedules]
                                                                     │
                                                                     ▼
[6. Create Work Order]  ◄── [5. Report Problem / Defect] ◄── [4. Record Inspections]
         │
         ▼
[7. Assign to Technician] ─► [8. Document Completed Work] ─► [9. Review History]
                                                                     │
                                                                     ▼
                                                          [10. Better Future Decisions]
```

### The 10-Step Maintenance Process

1. **Register Equipment (Asset Inventory)**  
   Every physical asset (such as a boiler, chiller, pump, or generator) is registered with its unique asset identification code, physical location, plant area, and operational criticality.

2. **Record Equipment Information (Technical Profile)**  
   The system captures essential baseline data, including manufacturer details, model numbers, serial plates, installation dates, rated capacities, design pressures, and operating limits.

3. **Create Maintenance Schedules (Preventive Planning)**  
   Maintenance managers establish calendar-based or runtime-based preventive maintenance routines (e.g., weekly lubrication, monthly inspections, annual overhauls) aligned with manufacturer recommendations and regulatory standards.

4. **Record Inspections and Daily Readings (Condition Monitoring)**  
   Shift operators use simple digital forms on mobile tablets or desktop computers to record routine walkthrough observations, gauge pressures, temperatures, and running hours.

5. **Report Equipment Problems (Defect Notification)**  
   Whenever an operator, technician, or facility occupant observes an anomaly—such as a steam leak, strange motor noise, or abnormal temperature—they can log a defect notification immediately with photos attached.

6. **Create Maintenance Work Orders (Formal Ticketing)**  
   Reported defects and scheduled preventive tasks are converted into formal work orders that detail the scope of work, required safety precautions, required spare parts, and priority levels.

7. **Assign Work to Technicians (Resource Allocation)**  
   Supervisors assign work orders to specific internal technicians or specialized external contractors based on availability, trade skills, and job urgency.

8. **Record Completed Work (Execution Documentation)**  
   Upon completing a task, the technician records actual labor hours spent, notes root causes, lists replacement parts consumed, and uploads photographic evidence of the completed repair.

9. **Review Maintenance History (Asset Timeline)**  
   All completed work orders, shift readings, and inspection notes are automatically appended to the asset's permanent service history, creating an unbroken audit trail.

10. **Use Information to Improve Future Decisions (Continuous Optimization)**  
    Maintenance managers review equipment reliability trends, recurring failure causes, and maintenance expenditure to fine-tune PM frequencies, adjust operating parameters, and forecast capital replacements.

> **Current Implementation vs. Future Capabilities**:  
> *Already Implemented*: Centralized asset registry, multi-facility organization management, interactive work order management, preventive maintenance scheduling, digital daily boiler logbook, and role-based access control.  
> *Planned on Future Roadmap*: Native offline mobile application with QR-code asset scanning, integrated spare parts inventory tracking, external contractor self-service portal, and automated utility billing analytics.

## 4. Why the Project Starts with Boiler Maintenance

While LuminaFM is designed as a comprehensive facility management platform, our initial deep specialist application focuses specifically on **industrial steam boilers**.

### The Critical Role of Steam Boilers in Facility Operations
In commercial hospitality, healthcare, and industrial manufacturing environments, the central steam boiler is often described as the "heart of the utility plant":
- **Vital Facility Services**: Boilers generate high-pressure steam essential for central laundry operations, domestic hot water distribution, commercial kitchen food preparation, and space heating. A boiler outage immediately degrades guest comfort and halts critical hotel services.
- **Extreme Operational and Safety Risks**: Steam boilers operate under high pressures (typically 8 to 9 bar in commercial hotels) and severe combustion temperatures. Neglected maintenance, valve failures, or water level cut-off malfunctions can lead to catastrophic pressure vessel explosions, structural destruction, and loss of life.
- **Massive Energy and Fuel Costs**: Industrial burners consume thousands of liters of heavy fuel oil or diesel daily. Thermodynamic research demonstrates that even a microscopic 1 mm layer of boiler scale on heat transfer tubes can increase fuel consumption by 5% to 8%, wasting millions of rupees in annual energy expenses.

### Realities of Developing and Low-Tech Building Operations
In many developing economies and mid-tier facilities, boiler plants operate without costly automated Internet-of-Things (IoT) sensors, automated chemical dosing skids, or supervisory SCADA telemetry. Instead:
- Plant operations rely entirely on human shift technicians who perform visual inspections and write gauge readings in paper logbooks every two hours.
- Maintenance is often reactive: minor symptoms are overlooked until an emergency breakdown forces an expensive plant shutdown.
- Water treatment and blowdown procedures are managed inconsistently, accelerating internal scaling and corrosion.

### The Research Foundation: Capturing 35 Validated Maintenance Interventions
The foundational research analyzed four thermodynamic systems comprising ten primary boiler components:
1. **Feed Water System**: Feed pump, economizer, and feed water tank.
2. **Steam Generation System**: Heat exchanger/fire tubes and boiler pressure shell.
3. **Fuel & Combustion System**: Combustion chamber, burner assembly, and exhaust stack.
4. **Safety & Control System**: Safety relief valves and master pressure gauges.

Through an iterative five-round validation process with deeply experienced industrial practitioners—including Chief Engineers, specialized steam engineering executives, and senior boiler operators with up to 52 years of hands-on experience—the research codified **35 essential maintenance activities** along with their normal operating benchmarks and anomaly thresholds.

By bringing this specialized boiler research into LuminaFM, we demonstrate how a general CMMS platform can provide deep, equipment-specific intelligence rather than generic, surface-level record keeping.

## 5. The Research-Based Decision Support Idea

One of the most innovative aspects of the LuminaFM project is its research-based approach to **maintenance decision support**, derived from the **Analytic Network Process (ANP)**.

To a non-technical facility professional, mathematical terminology like "multi-criteria decision-making" or "network supermatrices" can sound abstract and intimidating. In reality, the underlying concept reflects how an experienced Chief Engineer already thinks.

### An Everyday Analogy: How Experienced Engineers Make Decisions

> *Imagine you are a Chief Engineer arriving at your facility on Monday morning. Your maintenance supervisor presents you with several pressing issues across the boiler plant:*
> - *The burner nozzle shows signs of carbon buildup, and fuel consumption has crept up by 8%.*
> - *The feed water pump has logged 11,500 continuous running hours and is vibrating slightly.*
> - *The flue gas temperature is reading 198°C, which is 8°C higher than the normal operating baseline.*
> - *The safety relief valve is due for its mandatory semi-annual test next week.*
> 
> *Which task should your technicians tackle first?*
> 
> *To answer this, you do not simply look at one factor in isolation. You instinctively weigh multiple connected considerations simultaneously: Which issue poses an immediate life-safety hazard? Which one risks a catastrophic plant shutdown? Which one is actively wasting costly fuel? What does the equipment's recent repair history show?*

### Why Maintenance Involves Connected Factors
In real facility operations, equipment symptoms are intimately connected:
- A high flue gas temperature (above 190°C) does not exist in a vacuum; it may be caused by insulating soot on fire tubes, poor air-to-fuel calibration in the burner, or mineral scale insulating the water side of the boiler shell.
- Postponing feed pump bearing lubrication directly accelerates mechanical seal wear, which can cause feed pressure to drop below 4.0 bar, ultimately starving the boiler of water.

Traditional software systems treat maintenance tasks as independent checklists. The research-based decision model developed at the University of Moratuwa recognizes that **criteria and maintenance alternatives influence one another**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        DUAL-NETWORK DECISION LOGIC                     │
├────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┤
│    NETWORK 1: DAILY OPERATIONAL    │    NETWORK 2: PERIODIC LIFECYCLE  │
│          & CORRECTIVE TRIAGE       │          & PREVENTIVE CYCLES      │
├────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┤
│ • 2-Hourly Shift Gauge Logs        │ • Cumulative Running Hours        │
│ • Flue Gas Temperatures            │ • Calendar Intervals              │
│ • Steam & Feed Pressures           │ • Monthly / Quarterly Routines    │
│ • Sight Glass Water Levels         │ • Semi-Annual & Annual Overhauls  │
│                                    │                                   │
│                ▼                   │                 ▼                 │
│ Immediate Corrective Alerts &      │ Strategic Overhaul Planning &     │
│ Same-Shift Repair Work Orders      │ Scheduled Spare Parts Procurement │
└────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │     HUMAN-CENTERED DECISION RECOMMENDATION              │
        │ Prioritized maintenance advice presented clearly to the │
        │ Chief Engineer — who always retains final decision authority│
        └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Dual-Network Approach: Daily Triage vs. Long-Term Planning
The research structures boiler maintenance decisions into two interconnected tiers:
1. **Network 1 (Daily Operational & Corrective Triage)**: Evaluates 2-hourly logbook entries against empirical benchmarks (e.g., steam pressure below 8.0 bar, stack temperature above 190°C, ammeter surges). If an anomaly is detected, it triggers immediate shift alerts and recommends corrective actions before minor variances evolve into breakdown events.
2. **Network 2 (Periodic & Preventive Lifecycle)**: Tracks cumulative running hours (such as feed pump runtime approaching 12,000 hours or burner hours reaching 2,000 hours) alongside calendar intervals to schedule monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, and annual overhauls.

### Transparent Support, Not an "Automated Black Box"
We firmly believe that software should never attempt to replace the judgment, intuition, and legal responsibility of qualified engineers:
- **Decision Support, Not Decision Automation**: LuminaFM does not claim to magically predict the future or execute automated repairs. 
- **Explainable Recommendations**: When the system recommends that a burner nozzle inspection should take precedence over economizer cleaning, it presents the exact underlying reasons (e.g., "Elevated fuel consumption rate (+10%) and exhaust temperature above 195°C detected").
- **Engineer Retains Full Control**: The Chief Engineer reviews the recommendation, considers external operational priorities (such as high hotel occupancy or scheduled banquet events), and makes the final decision.

### Specialized Equipment Intelligence Modules
Different machines have fundamentally different physics, operating parameters, and failure modes. A generic maintenance form that treats a centrifugal chiller the same as a fire door fails to provide meaningful operational value.

Our architectural strategy pairs a **robust common CMMS core** with **specialized equipment intelligence modules**:

- **Core CMMS Engine (Universal to all assets)**:
  - Central asset hierarchies (Organization → Facility → Building → Floor → Room → Asset).
  - Standardized work order creation, approval, dispatch, and close-out.
  - Planned preventive maintenance (PPM) scheduling and recurring task generation.
  - Comprehensive audit logging, evidence photo uploads, and technician labor tracking.

- **Proposed Future Specialist Modules (Tailored equipment logic)**:
  - **Centrifugal & Screw Chillers**: Tracking refrigerant temperatures, evaporator approach temperatures, compressor motor winding temperatures, and condenser tube fouling.
  - **Emergency Diesel Generators**: Tracking battery cranking voltage drops, fuel filtration differential pressure, coolant jacket temperatures, and mandatory monthly load-bank testing.
  - **Pumping & Hydronic Systems**: Monitoring vibration signatures, mechanical seal leakage rates, suction/discharge differential pressures, and motor operating amperes.
  - **Air Handling Units (AHUs)**: Monitoring air filter differential pressure drops, supply/return air temperatures, belt tension status, and fan bearing temperatures.

### Flexibility Across Diverse Facilities
The LuminaFM architecture is being designed to support diverse operational contexts:
- **Five-Star Hotels & Resorts**: Ensuring 24/7 guest comfort, hot water reliability, and banquet readiness without disruptive utility outages.
- **Hospitals & Healthcare Facilities**: Meeting stringent clean-air standards, statutory compliance, and uninterrupted emergency power.
- **Commercial High-Rise Towers**: Managing decentralized HVAC equipment, elevator reliability, and tenant maintenance requests.
- **Manufacturing & Processing Plants**: Minimizing unplanned production line downtime and extending capital machinery lifespan.
